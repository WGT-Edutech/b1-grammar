Future Forms: will / going to / present continuous

B1

Three ways to talk about the future

English has several ways to talk about the future. They are not exactly the same, so the meaning changes depending on which form you choose.

will

Use for spontaneous decisions, promises, offers, predictions based on opinion.

It's cold in here. I'll close the window. I think it will rain later.
be going to

Use for plans or intentions, or predictions based on present evidence.

I'm going to study medicine. Look at those clouds. It is going to rain!
Present continuous for future

Use for definite personal arrangements. You usually say when.

I'm meeting Anna tomorrow at 6.
Quick guide

Spontaneous decision = will. Intention or prediction from evidence = going to. Fixed personal arrangement = present continuous.

Exercise 2 — Correct the mistake

Each sentence has ONE mistake. Rewrite it correctly.

1 I will call my mother later. (planned) → I'm going to call my mother later
2 The phone is ringing. I am going to get it. → I'll get it
3 She is going to have lunch with her boss on Tuesday. (fixed arrangement) → She is having lunch with her boss on Tuesday
4 Look! It will snow. (black clouds) → It is going to snow
5 I promise I am not going to tell anyone. → I won't tell anyone
6 They will arrive at 7 p.m. (confirmed plan) → They are arriving at 7 p.m.
7 I will be a doctor when I grow up. (intention) → I'm going to be a doctor when I grow up
8 I am helping you with this exercise. (spontaneous offer) → I'll help you with this exercise
